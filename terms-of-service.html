7. Paper Mode

7.1 Paper Mode simulates trading against live market data using notional capital. No real funds are at risk and no real orders are placed.

7.2 Paper Mode results are simulated. We build the simulated environment to reflect live market conditions as closely as we can: it runs against live market data and applies commission and slippage estimates calibrated to the venues we support. To keep the gap between simulated and live conditions as small as possible, we enable Paper Mode only on highly liquid venues and asset pairs, where execution conditions are most stable and most predictable.

Even so, a simulation is a model of the market, not the market. Some conditions cannot be reproduced in advance: exact queue position and fill sequencing, network and venue latency at the moment of an order, funding payments, partial fills, venue outages, and liquidity that thins unexpectedly. Simulated results therefore describe how an Agent performed under simulated conditions, and are not a representation of the result the same Agent would have produced, or will produce, in live markets.

14.4 If the Venue is unavailable at the moment of revocation, the Trading API Key is disabled immediately but positions may remain open at the Venue until it can accept orders. Positions that remain open remain exposed to the market and to liquidation by the Venue.

15.3 In extreme moves the Venue's own liquidation engine may close your position before your stop-loss order is filled. In that case the Venue's liquidation terms apply, the loss can extend to the whole of your Session Capital, and our fee disclosures and thresholds have no bearing on the outcome.

19.2 How the performance fee is calculated. Profit for this purpose means the increase in the value of the Session Capital from the start of the Session to the point of withdrawal, after deducting the runtime fee, the routing fee, and Venue trading fees and funding payments applicable to that Session.

Each Session is assessed on its own. Losses on one Session are not carried forward to offset profit on another. This means that if you lose on one Session and make a profit on the next, a performance fee is charged on that profit even though you may still be down overall. The performance fee is charged only on a Session that is in profit on the basis set out above; it is never charged on a Session that is flat or at a loss.

19.3 Fees are deducted as they fall due and appear on your Session receipt.

PART E — RISK DISCLOSURE STATEMENT

This Part is the Risk Disclosure Statement referred to in clause 1.2(a). You will be asked to confirm that you have read it before your first real-capital deployment. It is written to be read, not skimmed.

20. The central warning

Deploying an Agent with real capital can result in the loss of all of the capital you allocate. Do not deploy money you cannot afford to lose entirely. Automated trading carries substantial risk. Returns are not guaranteed, and most retail participants in leveraged digital-asset markets lose money.

21. Risks specific to automated agents

21.1 The Agent decides, and you cannot predict what it will do. An Agent trained by our factory has an internal policy that is not fully explainable. It may trade more often, hold longer, or take different positions than you expect. There is no human judgement applied to individual trades.

21.2 Model risk. An Agent is fitted to past data and past market regimes. It may be overfitted — that is, it may have learned patterns that were noise rather than signal. When market conditions change, an Agent that performed well can fail quickly and without warning.

21.3 Simulated results overstate live results. Paper Mode approximates costs and market impact. Live trading involves real queue position, real latency, real partial fills, real funding, and real liquidity. Expect live performance to be worse than simulated performance, sometimes substantially.

21.4 Ratings are backward-looking. See clause 11.3. A high ELO reflects past rounds in a defined environment. It is not a forecast.

21.5 Correlated failure. Many Agents on the Platform may hold similar positions at the same time. Diversifying across several Agents may provide less protection than it appears to.

21.6 Software and infrastructure failure. Bugs, model errors, connectivity failures, key-management failures, data-feed corruption and deployment errors can cause unintended trades, missed exits, duplicated orders or a failure to stop when instructed.

22. Risks of leverage and derivatives

22.1 Leverage multiplies losses as well as gains. At 2x, a 5% adverse move in the underlying is a 10% loss on your Session Capital. At 5x, a 20% adverse move can eliminate it.

22.2 Liquidation. If the position moves far enough against you, the Venue will liquidate it. Liquidation happens on the Venue's terms, at the Venue's chosen time, and may occur before your stop-loss order is filled. You may lose the entire Session Capital.

You will not owe more than the Session Capital. The Venues we support do not permit a Session to create a debt owed by you beyond the capital allocated to it.

22.3 Funding payments. Perpetual contracts involve periodic funding payments between long and short holders. These can be significant, are unpredictable, and accrue against you while a position is held.

22.4 Auto-deleveraging. Where a position is liquidated and cannot be closed in the market on terms the Venue's insurance fund can absorb, perpetual venues may close positions held by other users in order to balance the book.

This mechanism selects profitable positions, and a stop-loss does not protect you from it. A stop-loss limits what a losing position can cost you. Auto-deleveraging does the opposite: it can close a position that is winning, at a price the Venue sets, before your take-profit is reached. If it happens, your Session ends early and on terms neither you nor the Agent chose.

22.5 Gaps and thin markets. Digital-asset markets trade continuously and can move very fast. Prices can gap through your stop. Historic examples include single-day moves of more than 10% in major assets resulting in mass liquidations across the market.

23. Market, asset and counterparty risks

23.1 Volatility. Digital-asset prices are highly volatile and can fall to zero.

23.2 Venue risk. A Venue can halt trading, delist an instrument, suffer an outage, change its liquidation mechanics, restrict your jurisdiction, freeze your account, be hacked, or fail. Assets held at a Venue may be lost.
